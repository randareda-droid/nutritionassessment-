Your supplement plan

Chosen from your results and from how a gastric bypass changes absorption. Tap a card for the reasoning. Items marked “after questionnaire” depend on your answers.

Iron

30 mg elemental iron, once daily
START NOWMorning, empty stomach
Because: ferritin 19 ng/ml (below range), serum iron 55 ug/dl (low-normal), haemoglobin 13.6 g/dl and MCV 86 (still normal).
  • Form: ferrous bisglycinate (e.g. Ferropolyn, Ironglor-Max) or liposomal iron (e.g. Bioferrin). Both are gentler on the stomach than ferrous sulfate.
  • Take on waking with half an orange (about 60 g). The vitamin C improves absorption, which is reduced after a bypass.
  • Keep it 2 hours away from tea, coffee, dairy and calcium.
  • Together with your multivitamin, total iron should come to roughly 45–65 mg a day.
  • Recheck CBC and ferritin after 8–10 weeks. Continue until ferritin is at least 50 ng/ml, then your multivitamin takes over.
🌿

Bariatric multivitamin & mineral

One dose twice daily, with meals
START NOWBreakfast & dinner
Because: a bypass reduces absorption of many micronutrients at once, and several (A, E, K, copper, selenium, thiamine) were not measured.

Choose a bariatric-formulated product whose daily total provides about:

  • Folate 400–800 mcg, thiamine at least 12 mg
  • Zinc 8–22 mg with copper 2 mg (zinc without copper is not advised)
  • Vitamin A 5,000–10,000 IU (as beta-carotene or mixed), vitamin E 15 mg, vitamin K 90–120 mcg
  • Selenium 50–100 mcg

Chewable or liquid forms absorb better than hard tablets.

Thiamine (vitamin B1)

50 mg once daily, with lunch
PROTECTIVE
Because: thiamine was not tested, and deficiency after a bypass can develop quickly with vomiting or poor intake. It is water-soluble and very safe at this dose. A B-complex that supplies 50 mg of thiamine is fine.
🦴

Calcium citrate

500 mg per dose, up to 2–3 times daily
AFTER QUESTIONNAIRECitrate form only
Because: serum calcium is normal (9.3 mg/dl) and vitamin D is good. Whether you need a supplement depends on how much calcium you get from food. After a bypass the target is 1,200–1,500 mg a day in total.
  • Citrate, not carbonate. Citrate absorbs without stomach acid.
  • Never more than 500 mg at a time, and 2 hours apart from iron and the multivitamin.
  • Taken with meals, calcium also binds oxalate in the gut, which protects your kidneys and urinary tract.
🍗

Protein — food first

60–80 g a day, spread over 3 meals
DietShake after questionnaire
Because: total protein 6.7 g/dl sits right at the lower limit, although albumin (4.2) is healthy. Aim for about 20–25 g at each meal:
  • 2 large eggs ≈ 12 g
  • 100 g cooked chicken breast ≈ 30 g
  • 150 g grilled fish ≈ 30 g
  • 170 g plain Greek yogurt ≈ 15 g
  • ½ cup (100 g) cooked lentils ≈ 9 g

A protein shake is added only if food cannot reach 60 g. With your kidney markers slightly raised, I prefer food first and no large protein-powder loads until they are repeated.

Safety essentials

Please avoid for now

  • Anti-inflammatory painkillers (ibuprofen, diclofenac, naproxen). They strain the kidneys and can cause ulcers at the bypass join. Paracetamol is the safer choice.
  • High-dose vitamin C (above 100 mg a day from supplements). It raises oxalate and the risk of kidney stones.
  • Creatine, “pre-workout” blends and herbal detox products.
  • Potassium supplements and salt substitutes. Your potassium is 4.8, near the upper end.

Not needed — your levels are good

  • Extra vitamin D, beyond 2,000 IU
  • Extra B12 and folate
  • Extra zinc, and magnesium (Mg 2.2, normal)
  • Omega-3 for blood fats. Your LDL is 48 and triglycerides 50.
Go to the nearest hospital emergency department for: vomiting that lasts more than 24 hours, black or bloody stools, fainting, severe abdominal pain, blood in the urine, or being unable to pass urine.
